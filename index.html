Towards Intelligent Models

A Survey of Definitions, Measures and Implementations of Model Intelligence

Abstract

Intelligence is one of the most widely used but least clearly defined concepts in artificial intelligence. Although the field is built around the idea of intelligent machines, there is still little agreement on what intelligence is, how it should be measured, or what evidence is sufficient to claim that a system possesses it. This survey traces how these questions have been approached, from their origins in philosophy and psychometrics to contemporary machine learning. We organize the literature using a two-axis taxonomy that combines four fundamental questions, what intelligence is, whether machines can possess it, how it should be measured, and how it can be built, with eight major research programmes that answer these questions in different ways. Across these programmes, we review the main theories, the empirical evidence supporting and challenging them, and the evaluation methods used to study intelligent systems, including behavioural tests, formal and psychometric measures, benchmark-based evaluation, and the main strategies used to build intelligent systems. Looking across this literature reveals a recurring pattern that the claims about machine capabilities are often based on benchmark performance, later challenged by evidence that models rely on memorization or superficial cues, and eventually give way to a deeper question of whether the benchmark actually measures the intended capability. Stronger models may improve benchmark scores, but they do not answer that question. The survey concludes by discussing the implications for future evaluation and identifying directions for developing more reliable measures of machine intelligence.

Question III: Criterion — How Intelligence Is Measured

Any criterion answers two questions at once, whether a result counts as evidence and what exactly it is evidence for. The field has been far more confident about the first than the second.

Criterion Intrinsic difficulty Contamination-resistant Stated construct
Imitation game (Turing 1950) No No No
Legg-Hutter measure (Legg and Hutter 2007) Yes No Yes
Universal psychometrics (Hernandez-Orallo 2017) Partial No Partial
Skill-acquisition efficiency (ARC-AGI, Chollet 2019) Partial Yes No
Challenging benchmarks (LiveBench, HLE) No Partial No

Construct validity conditions satisfied by the criteria of this section. Each condition is met by at least one criterion, and none meets all three. The criterion that comes closest is the one that cannot be computed, and the benchmarks marketed as the most challenging supply the fewest conditions.
